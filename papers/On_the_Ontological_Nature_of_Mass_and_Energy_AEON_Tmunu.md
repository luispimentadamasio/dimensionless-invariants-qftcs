## 1. The Ontological Silence of Modern Physics and the Kant–Einstein Divide

A persistent rift separates mathematical physics from fundamental ontology. Physics excels at specifying functional relationships among measured quantities—how a trajectory bends, how a spectral line shifts, how a scattering cross-section scales—yet when pressed on the intrinsic nature of its most elementary referents, its definitions become conspicuously circular.

Consider the foundational triad of mechanics and relativity: *mass*, *energy*, and *time*. In standard treatments, energy is defined as "the capacity to do work," while work is defined as force integrated over displacement, force as the rate of change of momentum, and momentum as mass multiplied by velocity—where velocity presupposes a background coordinate parameter $t$ against which spatial change is measured. Mass, in turn, is characterized either through its dynamical effects (inertial resistance $F/a$ or gravitational coupling) or as an intrinsic numerical parameter assigned to fields in a Lagrangian. Even Einstein’s celebrated equivalence,

$$E = h\nu = m_0 c^2,$$

is almost universally interpreted as a quantitative conversion rule linking three distinct ontological categories: *energy* (dynamic activity), *frequency* (periodic rate), and *mass* (material substance).

For the metaphysician, this leaves the central riddle untouched. If mass is conceived as a passive, persistent substrate (*substance*) and energy as an active capacity or flux (*process*), how can inert stuff be "converted" into pure activity during nuclear fission or particle–antimatter annihilation? Conversely, if everything is reduced to mathematical formalism without an ontological referent, physics forfeits any account of what exists in reality.

This conceptual impasse is reinforced by a deeper historical division in the philosophy of time:
1. **Kant’s Transcendental Aesthetic (1781):** Immanuel Kant recognized that time and space are not empirical objects floating inside the world, but the *a priori* constitutive conditions that make sequence, relationality, and structured phenomena possible—forming an unbroken **three-way infinity** (endless past, endless future, and infinitely divisible depth). Yet to avoid Newtonian and Leibnizian antinomies, Kant confined that constitutive architecture strictly to the transcendental subject (*objects-as-experienced-by-us*), leaving the *thing-in-itself* (*object-as-object*) outside time and space.
2. **Einstein’s Relativistic Metrology (1905–1915):** Albert Einstein demonstrated experimentally that what physical clocks measure *in situ* is local, relational, and dynamically coupled to mass-energy and motion ($d\tau = \sqrt{N^2}\,dt$). Yet by absorbing time into a four-dimensional geometric manifold ($g_{\mu\nu}$), modern physics spatialized becoming into a static "block universe," stripping time of its active, constitutive agency.

This treatise argues that both impasses dissolve through a single foundational shift: **reifying Kant’s transcendental aesthetic by placing the constitutive architecture of time directly inside reality itself as the ontological ground of every object-as-object.**
