\begin{abstract}
Bell's theorem (1964) demonstrates that no physical theory grounded in classical local hidden variables can reproduce all statistical predictions of quantum mechanics. Within the philosophy of physics and quantum foundations, this result is frequently interpreted as creating a fundamental tension between quantum entanglement and relativistic locality. In this paper, we argue that Bell's theorem is completely vindicated in its mathematical and empirical scope, and that the apparent tension arises from conflating quantum correlations with an idealized, infinite-precision \emph{naive classical point-locality}. Extending the Operational Simultaneity Horizon ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}$) formalism developed in earlier installments of this series, we introduce \textbf{Coherence-Bounded Operational Locality}. We prove that while post-selected Bell--CHSH correlations depend solely on local phase coherence $\Phi_\tau(\Omega)$ independent of spatial separation, active simultaneous phase synchronization across a physical channel is strictly bounded by the round-trip signal propagation time $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}}$ relative to the system's phase decoherence rate $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$. We derive a dimensionless \textbf{Spatial Locality Decay Factor} ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}$) that cleanly separates passive non-local quantum correlations from active operational causal control. Finally, we propose a concrete experimental protocol---the \textbf{Delayed-Feedback Entangled Optical Clock Experiment}---spanning optical fiber baselines from $\Delta\ell = 10\,\mathrm{m}$ to $50\,\mathrm{km}$ under tunable environmental noise $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ to verify this divergence empirically.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: Re-examining Locality After Bell}

Since John Stewart Bell's landmark 1964 theorem and its subsequent loophole-free experimental confirmations, debates over the foundations of quantum mechanics have centered on the compatibility of quantum non-locality with relativistic spacetime structure. Within standard four-dimensionalist or Block Universe accounts of relativity, spacetime events occupy fixed coordinates on a Lorentzian manifold where causal influence is strictly restricted to the light cone. Against this backdrop, the violation of the Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH) inequality ($S_{\mathrm{CHSH}} > 2$) across spacelike-separated wings is often framed as ``spooky action at a distance'' or a breakdown of spatial locality.

However, a rigorous operational distinction must be drawn between:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Correlation across spacelike intervals:} Statistical dependencies revealed through post-selected classical coincidence records.
    \item \textbf{Operational causal synchronization:} The physical establishment, verification, and maintenance of a shared phase reference between distant local laboratories in real time.
\end{enumerate}

Bell's theorem definitively rules out local hidden-variable models that assume classical point-like properties exist independently of measurement contexts. It does not, however, imply that physical observers can transmit actionable phase information superluminally, nor does it render spatial separation physically irrelevant to metrological coordination. What requires refinement is not Bell's theorem, which remains completely vindicated, but rather the unexamined classical assumption of \textbf{naive point-locality}---the idealization that physical systems possess infinitely sharp, zero-latency coordinate states independent of the finite thermodynamic and electromagnetic channels required to synchronize them.

\section{Theoretical Framework: Coherence-Bounded Operational Locality}

To formalize the boundary between passive quantum correlation and active relativistic synchronization, we analyze a bipartite entangled optical clock network separated by a proper physical baseline $\Delta\ell$ within a refractive medium of effective group index $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (such as standard telecom optical fiber, where $N_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 1.468$).

\subsection{Round-Trip Signal Delay and the Operational Simultaneity Horizon}

Establishing operational simultaneity between two spatially separated nodes $A$ and $B$ requires a closed two-way electromagnetic exchange (Einstein--Poincar\'{e} synchronization). The minimum round-trip signal delay $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}}$ across baseline $\Delta\ell$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}} = \frac{2 \Delta\ell}{c N_{\mathrm{eff}}}
    \label{eq:roundtrip}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum and $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ characterizes the effective refractive transit parameter of the channel.

During this round-trip interval $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}}$, both local oscillators and the quantum channel experience total phase decoherence at rate $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ (in $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$), encompassing thermal drift, acoustic phase noise, and quantum projection noise. Let $\rho_\tau \in (0, 1]$ denote the dimensionless phase-locking threshold parameter required to sustain a valid operational simultaneity frame.

\begin{definition}[Operational Simultaneity Horizon]
Active phase synchronization between two nodes remains operationally valid if and only if the round-trip signal delay does not exceed the effective coherence window $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}} = \rho_\tau / \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$. Setting $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}} = \tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$ yields the Operational Simultaneity Horizon:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}} = \frac{c N_{\mathrm{eff}} \rho_\tau}{2 \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}}
    \label{eq:lsim}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Beyond $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}$, closed-loop phase feedback cannot converge faster than stochastic phase diffusion erases the shared reference frame.

\subsection{Operational Simultaneity Fidelity vs.\ Post-Selected CHSH Correlations}

Consider an entangled photon-pair source distributing time-bin or polarization-entangled states correlated with local optical clock transitions driven at coupling parameter $\Omega$. Let $\Phi_\tau(\Omega) \in [0, 1]$ denote the intrinsic local temporal coherence function of the entangled clock states prior to spatial channel transit.

When nodes $A$ and $B$ perform local projective measurements and compare timestamps \emph{post-hoc} via classical channels, the resulting \textbf{post-selected CHSH Bell correlation} depends purely on the preserved state purity $\Phi_\tau(\Omega)$:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}(\Omega) = 2\sqrt{2} \cdot \Phi_\tau(\Omega)
    \label{eq:chsh}
\end{equation}
Provided $\Phi_\tau(\Omega) > 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.7071$, the system violates the classical Bell bound ($S_{\mathrm{CHSH}} > 2$) independent of the physical baseline $\Delta\ell$.

Conversely, when nodes $A$ and $B$ utilize the channel for \textbf{real-time, active operational phase synchronization} without post-selection, the actionable \textbf{Operational Simultaneity Fidelity} ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}}$) decays exponentially with the ratio of the physical baseline to the Operational Simultaneity Horizon:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}}(\Omega, \Delta\ell) = \Phi_\tau(\Omega) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta\ell}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}}\right)
    \label{eq:ssim}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Spatial Locality Decay Factor}

\begin{proposition}[Separation of Correlation and Operational Synchronization]
The normalized ratio between Operational Simultaneity Fidelity and post-selected CHSH Bell correlation isolates a pure spatial-causal decay law independent of intrinsic state preparation purity $\Phi_\tau(\Omega)$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} = \frac{2\sqrt{2} \cdot \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}}(\Omega, \Delta\ell)}{S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}(\Omega)} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta\ell}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}}\right) = \exp\left(-\frac{2 \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}} \Delta\ell}{c N_{\mathrm{eff}} \rho_\tau}\right)
    \label{eq:dloc}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\section{Experimental Proposal: Delayed-Feedback Entangled Optical Clock Test}

To test Coherence-Bounded Operational Locality empirically, we propose the \textbf{Delayed-Feedback Entangled Optical Clock Experiment}.

\subsection{Apparatus Architecture}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Entangled Optical Clock Nodes ($A$ and $B$):} Two trapped-ion or optical-lattice clock ensembles ($^{87}\mathrm{Sr}$ or $^{171}\mathrm{Yb}^+$) prepared in a shared Bell state via remote photon-mediated entanglement, maintaining intrinsic coherence $\Phi_\tau(\Omega) \ge 0.92$.
    \item \textbf{Variable Optical Fiber Baseline ($\Delta\ell$):} Spools of dispersion-shifted telecom fiber ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.468$) providing discrete physical baselines $\Delta\ell \in \{10\,\mathrm{m}, 100\,\mathrm{m}, 1\,\mathrm{km}, 10\,\mathrm{km}, 50\,\mathrm{km}\}$.
    \item \textbf{Tunable Noise Injection ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$):} Controlled electro-optic phase-modulating noise generators coupled to the local clock lasers, sweeping $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ continuously from $10^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ (ultra-stable cryogenic baseline) to $10^5\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ (high-decoherence regime).
    \item \textbf{Dual Readout Channels:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Channel 1 (Post-Selected CHSH):} Time-tagged single-photon detectors recording coincidence events across randomized measurement bases to evaluate $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}(\Omega)$.
        \item \emph{Channel 2 (Closed-Loop Operational Sync):} Real-time phase-error feedback loop locking Clock $B$ to Clock $A$ across $\Delta\ell$ without post-selection, directly measuring $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}}(\Omega, \Delta\ell)$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantitative Predictions}

For a benchmark metrological configuration ($\rho_\tau = 0.5$, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.468$, $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}} = 5.0 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$), Eq.~\eqref{eq:lsim} yields:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}} = \frac{(2.9979 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m/s})(1.468)(0.5)}{2(5.0 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1})} \approx 22.0\,\mathrm{km}
\end{equation}
Across the discrete fiber baselines, the protocol predicts a decisive divergence between $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{At $\Delta\ell = 10\,\mathrm{m}$:} $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \approx 0.9995$. Both $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}} \approx 2.60$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}} \approx 0.92$ exhibit near-ideal performance.
    \item \textbf{At $\Delta\ell = 10\,\mathrm{km}$:} $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \approx 0.635$. $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}$ remains constant at $\approx 2.60$, whereas $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ decreases to $\approx 0.58$.
    \item \textbf{At $\Delta\ell = 50\,\mathrm{km}$:} $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \approx 0.103$. $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}$ remains robustly above the classical limit ($S_{\mathrm{CHSH}} \approx 2.60 > 2$), while real-time operational simultaneity collapses ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}} \approx 0.095$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Philosophical Implications for Spacetime and Locality}

Within a neutral four-dimensionalist ontology, all measurement events at $A$ and $B$ occupy fixed coordinates on the spacetime manifold. Bell's theorem proves that the joint probability distribution of these events cannot be factorized into local classical hidden states assigned to individual worldlines.

Coherence-Bounded Operational Locality demonstrates why this non-factorizability never threatens relativistic causality. Physical locality is not an abstract geometric postulate of zero-volume mathematical points; it is an \emph{operational capacity} governed by finite signal propagation ($\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}}$) and thermodynamic stability ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}$). By replacing naive classical point-locality with $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} = \exp(-\Delta\ell / \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}})$, quantum mechanics and special relativity coexist without contradiction: entanglement reflects global structural consistency across spacetime, while operational synchronization remains strictly bounded by the light cone and the arrow of decoherence.

\section{Conclusion}

Bell's theorem stands completely vindicated as a foundational pillar of modern physics. What collapses under experimental scrutiny is not relativistic locality, but the classical idealization of instantaneous, zero-noise point-locality. Through the Operational Simultaneity Horizon $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ and the Spatial Locality Decay Factor $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, we establish a testable, quantitative bridge between quantum information theory and relativistic metrology.
