\begin{abstract}
Uniting curved-spacetime quantum field theory (QFTCS), open-system quantum decoherence, and finite-speed clock synchronization within a single coupled numerical state exposes structural boundary tensions that remain hidden when relativistic geometry and quantum information are treated in isolation. By stress-testing the \texttt{AEON-T14.html} computational observatory across extreme kinematic, strong-field, and long-baseline regimes, we identify \textbf{five emergent edge-case paradoxes}: (1) an operational divergence between gravitational and kinematic time dilation across finite spatial baselines; (2) non-reciprocal simultaneity horizons between oscillators with unequal noise floors; (3) simultaneous temporal phase freezing and spatial locality collapse at stretched black hole horizons; (4) nonlinear thermal--curvature runaway near the semiclassical Kugelblitz limit; and (5) an apparent post-selection loophole in which Bell-CHSH violations persist beyond active synchronization bounds. We demonstrate that four of these five tensions admit \textbf{exact, closed-form analytical resolutions} derived directly from the framework's internal equations---recovering the Weak Equivalence Principle in the point-local limit, establishing symmetric mutual synchronization graphs, identifying a saddle-node bifurcation cliff for radiation back-reaction, and quantifying the minimum Shannon timestamp-alignment entropy ($I_{\mathrm{sync}}$) required to post-select nonlocal correlations. For the fifth tension, we prove a lapse-invariant \textbf{Operational Complementarity Identity} ($\rho_\tau \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r) = \mathrm{const.}$) while delineating the remaining open frontier in microscopic black hole quantum gravity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: Stress-Testing Coupled Operational Spacetime}

\subsection{How Emergent Boundary Tensions Arise}
In standard theoretical physics, general relativity and quantum information theory rely on mutually incompatible idealizations. General relativity presupposes point-local observers equipped with infinitely precise, decoherence-free clocks and classical light signals, whereas quantum information protocols typically evaluate entanglement across flat, static background foliations without accounting for gravitational redshift or round-trip phase-locking latency.

Building on the operational spacetime program established in Papers 1--13 \cite{damasio_compendium,damasio_p9,damasio_p10} and implemented computationally in \texttt{AEON-T14.html} \cite{damasio_aeont14}, forcing both sectors into a single self-consistent state vector reveals \textbf{five structural boundary tensions}. Crucially, these phenomena are neither numerical artifacts nor ad hoc modeling errors; rather, they are unavoidable physical consequences of evaluating open quantum oscillators inside curved, back-reacting Kerr--Newman geometries across finite baselines $\Delta\ell > 0$.

\subsection{Methodological Approach to Resolution}
Rather than patching edge cases with external assumptions, our methodology interrogates the internal algebra of the coupled observatory equations at their asymptotic boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Boundary Isolation:} Using reproducible state snapshots in \texttt{AEON-T14.html}, we isolate each tension by holding macroscopic observables ($\rho_\tau$ or $\Delta\ell$) constant while varying the underlying geometric or thermodynamic channel.
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Asymptotic Reduction:} We take exact analytical limits ($\Delta\ell \to 0$, $N^2(\theta) \to 0$, $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} \to \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{hor}}$) to test whether classical relativistic theorems are recovered continuously and to derive exact deviation residuals.
    \item \textbf{Classification of Resolved vs.\ Open Regimes:} We separate tensions that are completely closed by classical information theory, graph symmetry, or bifurcation analysis from regimes that genuinely interface with unsolved problems in non-perturbative quantum gravity.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Tensions 1 \& 2: Equivalence Breakdown and Asymmetric Horizons}

\subsection{Tension 1: Finite-Baseline Operational Equivalence Breakdown}
\textbf{The Problem:} In Einstein's Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP), a stationary clock in a gravitational potential with ZAMO lapse $N^2(\theta) < 1$ and an inertial clock moving in flat Minkowski space ($N^2 = 1$) with Lorentz factor $\gamma_{\mathrm{pre}} = 1/\sqrt{N^2(\theta)}$ share the identical proper-time dilation factor $\rho_\tau = 1/\sqrt{N^2(\theta)} = \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}$. Across a finite baseline $\Delta\ell > 0$, however, their \textbf{Operational Simultaneity Horizons} diverge:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r) = \frac{c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}} \, \left(\sqrt{N^2(\theta)} / \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}\right)}{2 \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}}, \qquad \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}} = \Gamma_{\mathrm{th}} + \Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}} + \Gamma_{\mathrm{tech}}
\end{equation}
While $\sqrt{N^2(\theta)} / \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}$ enters the numerator symmetrically, gravitational compactness $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} > 0$ activates Penrose--Di\'osi self-energy decoherence $\Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}} = (M_{\mathrm{eff}}/M_P)^2 (c/L)\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{DP}}$ \cite{diosi1989,penrose1996}, whereas flat-space motion ($\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0$) yields $\Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}} = 0$, giving $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{grav}} < \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{kin}}$ at identical $\rho_\tau$.

\textbf{Exact Resolution:} Taking the horizon ratio defines the \textbf{Operational Equivalence Deviation Factor} ($\eta_{\mathrm{WEP}}$), and expanding $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} = \exp(-\Delta\ell / \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}})$ for $\Delta\ell \ll \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ yields the \textbf{Finite-Baseline Equivalence Residual}:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{WEP}} \equiv \frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{grav}}}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{kin}}} = \left(1 + \frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}}}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{th}} + \Gamma_{\mathrm{tech}}}\right)^{-1}, \qquad \Delta\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{WEP}} \approx \frac{2 \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}} \, \Delta\ell}{c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}} \, \left(\sqrt{N^2(\theta)} / \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}\right)}
\end{equation}
In the point-local limit ($\Delta\ell \to 0$) or massless test-particle limit ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{grav}} \to 0$), $\Delta\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{WEP}} \to 0$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{WEP}} \to 1$, recovering Einstein's Weak Equivalence Principle continuously.

\subsection{Tension 2: Asymmetric Simultaneity Horizons}
\textbf{The Problem:} Because $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)$ depends on local oscillator decoherence $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(i)}$ and boost $\gamma_i$, a low-noise cryogenic clock (Node A) and a high-noise oscillator (Node B) separated by $\Delta\ell$ see non-reciprocal horizons ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(A)} \neq \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(B)}$), making simultaneity an asymmetric directed relation when $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(B)} < \Delta\ell < \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(A)}$.

\textbf{Exact Resolution:} Closed two-way phase synchronization succeeds if and only if \textbf{both} oscillators remain coherent across the round-trip interval ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mathrm{mutual}} = \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{(A)} \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{(B)}$), uniquely defining the \textbf{Symmetric Mutual Simultaneity Horizon}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{mutual}}(A,B) \equiv \left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(A)}} + \frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(B)}}\right)^{-1} = \frac{c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2 \left(\frac{\gamma_A \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(A)}}{\sqrt{N_A^2}} + \frac{\gamma_B \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}^{(B)}}{\sqrt{N_B^2}}\right)}
\end{equation}
This relation satisfies $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{mutual}}(A,B) \equiv \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{mutual}}(B,A)$ identically, restoring an undirected symmetric network graph for two-way locking while preserving one-way master-to-slave timing up to $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(A)}$.
\section{Tensions 3 \& 4: Stretched Horizons and Semiclassical Stability}

\subsection{Tension 3: Stretched-Horizon Operational Complementarity}
\textbf{The Problem:} As an infalling clock approaches a Kerr--Newman stretched horizon ($N^2(\theta) \to 10^{-6}$), proper-time dilation $\rho_\tau = \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}} / \sqrt{N^2(\theta)}$ diverges relative to an asymptotic lab observer, freezing local decoherence action $\Omega = \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}} t_{\mathrm{pre}}$ per unit lab time ($\Phi_\tau(\Omega) \to 1$). Simultaneously, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r) \propto \sqrt{N^2(\theta)} \to 0$, collapsing spatial locality decay $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \to 0$ across any $\Delta\ell > 0$.

\textbf{Exact Resolution:} Multiplying $\rho_\tau$ (referenced to an asymptotic flat lab frame) by $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)$ cancels $\sqrt{N^2(\theta)}$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}$ identically:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_\tau \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r) = \left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{pre}}}{\sqrt{N^2(\theta)}}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}} \, \sqrt{N^2(\theta)}}{2 \, \gamma_{\mathrm{pre}} \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}}\right) = \frac{c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2 \, \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}} \equiv \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(0)}
\end{equation}
This \textbf{Operational Complementarity Identity} proves that temporal freezing and spatial horizon collapse are conjugate aspects of a conserved flat-space coherence length $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}^{(0)}$: as $N^2(\theta) \to 0$, the stretched membrane preserves unitary temporal phase memory ($\Phi_\tau$) by sacrificing spatial reach ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}} \to 0$), fragmenting space into causally isolated membrane cells without destroying quantum information.

\subsection{Tension 4: Semiclassical Kugelblitz Stability Cliff}
\textbf{The Problem:} Under self-consistent radiation back-reaction ($G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G \langle T_{\mu\nu} \rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}$), gravitational strain raises effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}$, increasing Stefan--Boltzmann energy density $\rho_{\mathrm{th}} \propto T_{\mathrm{eff}}^4$ and feeding back into effective compactness $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathcal{C}_0 + \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}})$. Near the Buchdahl/Kugelblitz threshold, small entropy perturbations trigger runaway horizon collapse ($\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{hor}}$).

\textbf{Exact Resolution:} Expanding radiation compactness in powers of $\Delta\mathcal{C} \equiv \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{eff}} - \mathcal{C}_0$ as $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\Delta\mathcal{C}) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \Delta\mathcal{C} + \beta_2 (\Delta\mathcal{C})^2$ yields the quadratic fixed-point equation $\beta_2 (\Delta\mathcal{C})^2 - (1 - \beta_1)\Delta\mathcal{C} + \beta_0 = 0$, whose stable physical branch is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{C}_* = \frac{(1 - \beta_1) - \sqrt{(1 - \beta_1)^2 - 4\beta_0\beta_2}}{2\beta_2}
\end{equation}
Static semiclassical equilibria exist if and only if $(1 - \beta_1)^2 \ge 4\beta_0\beta_2$ and $\mathcal{C}_0 + \Delta\mathcal{C}_* < \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{hor}}(a_*, Q_*)$. Beyond the saddle-node bifurcation cliff $\Delta\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{crit}} = (1 - \beta_1)/(2\beta_2)$, runaway Kugelblitz collapse is mathematically guaranteed.

\section{Tension 5: The Post-Selection Synchronization Tax}

\textbf{The Problem:} Beyond $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)$ ($\Delta\ell \gg \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}$), active sync fidelity vanishes ($\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{sim}} = \Phi_\tau \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \to 0$), whereas post-selected CHSH correlations $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}(\Omega) = 2\sqrt{2}\,\Phi_\tau(\Omega)$ remain intact \cite{chsh1969}. Experimentally, pairing entangled photon timestamps across unsynchronized clocks requires overcoming oscillator drift accumulated over round-trip delay $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}} = 2\Delta\ell / (c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}})$.

\textbf{Exact Resolution:} Comparing $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{sig}}$ against local coherence time $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}} = 2\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r) / (c \, n_{\mathrm{eff}})$ yields $N_{\mathrm{bins}} = 1 + \Delta\ell / \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)$ phase-drift bins. By Shannon's source-coding theorem, recovering coincidence peaks requires exchanging at least $I_{\mathrm{sync}}$ classical bits per window:
\begin{equation}
    I_{\mathrm{sync}}(\Delta\ell, r) \ge \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{\Delta\ell}{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)}\right) = \log_2\!\left(1 - \ln\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}\right) \quad \mathrm{bits}
\end{equation}
Inside $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sim}}(r)$ ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \to 1$), $I_{\mathrm{sync}} \to 0$; beyond it ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{loc}} \to 0$), $I_{\mathrm{sync}}$ grows logarithmically, proving that post-selecting $S_{\mathrm{CHSH}} > 2$ always carries an explicit classical timing cost and never violates causal signaling bounds.

\section{Open Frontiers and Future Work}

While Tensions 1, 2, 4, and 5 are closed analytically within semiclassical operational spacetime, three frontiers remain open for future theoretical and experimental work:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Microscopic Horizon Microstates (Beyond Tension 3):} Explaining how stretched-horizon microstates encode nonlocal Page-curve correlations \cite{mss2016} without violating entanglement monogamy requires non-perturbative quantum gravity.
    \item \textbf{Full Radial Semiclassical TOV Integration (Beyond Tension 4):} Extending the single-zone quadratic fixed-point condition $\Delta\mathcal{C}_*$ to radially stratified semiclassical Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff profiles will test whether anisotropic vacuum tension ($p_r < 0$) stabilizes horizonless shells just inside the Buchdahl limit.
    \item \textbf{Satellite-to-Ground Optical Clock Bell Tests (Testing Tensions 1 \& 5):} Orbiting optical lattice clocks linked to ground stations offer an experimental testbed to measure both the finite-baseline equivalence residual $\Delta\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{WEP}}$ and classical timestamp-alignment entropy $I_{\mathrm{sync}}$.
\end{enumerate}
