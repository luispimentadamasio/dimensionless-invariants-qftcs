ontological conservation and allocation principle: the **Cadence

## 1. The Ontological Silence of Modern Physics

A persistent divide separates mathematical physics from fundamental
ontology. Physics excels at specifying functional relationships among
measured quantities---how a trajectory bends, how a spectral line
shifts, how a cross-section scales---yet when pressed on the intrinsic
nature of its most elementary referents, its definitions become
conspicuously circular.

Consider the foundational triad of mechanics and relativity: *mass*,
*energy*, and *time*. In standard pedagogical and foundational
treatments, energy is defined as "the capacity to do work," while work
is defined as force integrated over displacement, force as the rate of
change of momentum, and momentum as mass multiplied by velocity---where
velocity itself presupposes a pre-existing, background coordinate
parameter $t$ against which spatial change is measured. Mass, in turn,
is characterized either through its dynamical effects (inertial
resistance $F/a$ or gravitational coupling) or as an intrinsic numerical
parameter assigned to fields in a Lagrangian. Even Einstein's celebrated
equivalence,

$$E = h\nu = m_0 c^2,$$

is almost universally interpreted as a quantitative conversion rule
linking three distinct ontological categories: *energy* (dynamic
activity), *frequency* (periodic rate), and *mass* (material substance).

For the metaphysician, this leaves the central riddle untouched. If mass
is conceived as a passive, persistent substrate (*substance*) and energy
as an active capacity or flux (*process*), how can inert stuff be
"converted" into pure activity during nuclear fission or
particle--antiparticle annihilation? Conversely, if everything is
reduced to pure mathematical formalism without an ontological referent,
physics degenerates into instrumentalist bookkeeping---predicting
instrument readings while forfeiting any account of what exists in
reality.

This treatise argues that the impasse arises from a single inverted
premise: **treating time as a passive, empty container ($t$) within
which substances persist and energies flow, rather than recognizing
Objective Time as the active ontological ground of existence itself.**
